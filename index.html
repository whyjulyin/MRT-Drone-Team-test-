01 OVERVIEW /

Introduction

The University Rover Challenge (URC) is a robotics competition for university students, organized annually by the Mars Society near the Mars Desert Research Station (MDRS) in the desert of southern Utah, United States. The competition tasks student teams with designing and building next-generation Mars exploration rovers capable of executing field missions that simulate real planetary surface operations.

Field performance is evaluated across 4 distinct missions: Delivery, Autonomy, Equipment Servicing, and Astrobiology. Each mission is worth 100 points for a maximum achievable score of 400 mission points, though the drone can only be utilized for the Delivery and Autonomy missions. The Delivery Mission requires rovers to retrieve and deliver objects weighing up to 5 kg across challenging terrain over distances up to 1 km. The Autonomy Mission demands full autonomous navigation through waypoints as well as astronaut assistance and tool retrieval tasks without any human teleoperation. Overall team standings combine field mission points with engineering evaluations from the System Acceptance Review (SAR), which is submitted prior to the field competition.

Project Overview & Problem Statement

For this competition, our rover is expected to traverse undulating terrain at an estimated distance of up to 2 km. This introduces the risk of communication loss between the rover and our base station due to signal attenuation over long ranges and obstructed line-of-sight due to natural obstacles. As such, the aerial drone subsystem is deployed specifically during the Delivery Mission and Autonomy Mission to support ground operations.

Serving as a Non-Line-of-Sight (NVLOS) RF relay operating at an elevated hover altitude of 60 m AGL, the drone acts as an airborne repeater bridging primary telemetry and video links between the Command and Control base station and the ground rover when line-of-sight is severed by canyon walls and terrain ridges. Additionally, the drone provides aerial reconnaissance by scouting ahead of the ground rover to identify optimal driving paths, locate scattered mission equipment, and detect elevated ArUco markers beyond the field of view of ground-level cameras.

Operating from an enclosed base station

A proposed 20 m LMR400 antenna extension introduces estimated RF losses of 4.36 dB at 2.4 GHz or 2.56 dB at 900 MHz, before connector effects.
